\begin{frame}{Matrix on Apple M3 Max (regular = baseline)}
    {\footnotesize Size 360; 3 runs; 6 concurrent processes.
    Negative \% = faster than regular \texttt{new}.}

    \vspace{0.4em}
    \begin{center}
    {\scriptsize
    \begin{tabular}{@{}lrrr@{}}
        \toprule
        Allocator & Short & Medium & Long \\
        \midrule
        Regular new & 0\% & 0\% & 0\% \\
        \textbf{v1} & \(\mathbf{-3.83\%}\) & \(-3.48\%\) & \(\mathbf{-3.80\%}\) \\
        \textbf{v2} & \(-3.79\%\) & \(\mathbf{-3.49\%}\) & \(-3.79\%\) \\
        v3 & \(-2.97\%\) & \(-2.62\%\) & \(-2.93\%\) \\
        v6 & \(-2.82\%\) & \(-2.40\%\) & \(-2.77\%\) \\
        v4 & \(-2.51\%\) & \(-2.11\%\) & \(-2.47\%\) \\
        v7 & \(+1.76\%\) & \(+1.74\%\) & \(+1.77\%\) \\
        v5 & \(+2.66\%\) & \(+2.99\%\) & \(+2.50\%\) \\
        \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}
    }
    \end{center}

    \vspace{0.5em}
    \textbf{Takeaway:} v1/v2 win this matrix campaign.
    v5/v7 (cache-heavy) are \emph{slower} than regular here.
